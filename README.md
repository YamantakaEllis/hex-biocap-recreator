# hex-biocap-recreator

Open hypothesis for a sealed hexagonal micropore bioreactor cap to guide limb regeneration via chemotaxis, needle mode and saturation mode.

> This is a hypothesis for testing, not medical advice. Open to collaborators. MIT Licensed. Please credit the original idea if you build on it.

## The Idea in One Line

Don't let the wound scab. Keep it moist, fed, and electrically alive, and steer regrowth with food crumbs.

## Core Hypothesis

Mammalian limbs fail to regenerate because the wound is allowed to dry, scab, and lose bioelectric continuity, and because the regenerating tissue is forced to cannibalise systemic resources.

If the wound is maintained in a moist, ionically conductive, externally fed state, endogenous DNA patterning can resume, with growth guided by local nutrient gradients rather than top-down design.

## System Components

**1. Sealed Bioreactor Cap**
A flexible, sealed cap over the amputation site, like a shower cap. It maintains a moist, sterile microenvironment and prevents scab formation.

**2. Circulation Loop**
Formula is continuously circulated through an external dialysis-type filtration unit. Waste is removed, pathogens are filtered, fresh nutrients are reintroduced. This maintains sterility over long durations.

**3. Conductive Ion Medium**
The internal fluid contains sodium, potassium, calcium, and other ions to sustain a conductive pathway. This allows endogenous bioelectric fields to loop outside the body through the medium and re-enter, rather than being blocked by dry scab tissue.

**4. Nutrient Medium**
The fluid supplies calcium for bone, amino acids for muscle, glucose and oxygen for metabolism. This prevents systemic depletion during regrowth. External supply replaces internal cannibalisation.

## Hexagonal Micropore Array

The inner surface of the cap is a honeycomb array of micropores. Each pore is surrounded by 6 neighbouring pores.

This geometry provides uniform tiling with no gaps, 6 possible growth directions per pore, and predictable propagation of local signals.

The same hardware supports two delivery modes.

## Two Delivery Modes

**Needle Mode**
Precise, single-pore delivery. Used for linear structures: nerves, blood vessels, and bone scaffolds. A carbon-based biocompatible scaffold is deposited along the needle path to provide temporary mechanical structure for bone cells.

**Saturation Mode**
Multi-pore delivery. 7, 19, 37, or more pores fire together to flood a patch. Used for bulk tissue: muscle, fat, and skin. Saturation fills tissue around pre-established needle lines.

Same hex caps, different pattern.

## AI Control and the Crumb Rule

The AI does not design the limb. DNA contains the patterning information.

The AI acts as a closed-loop controller: sense, decide, deliver, sense again.

Sensors monitor pH, ion concentration, oxygen, metabolites, and growth activity at the wound face.

Control is achieved by chemotaxis, called the Crumb Rule. Cells and growth cones follow nutrient gradients. By placing a small nutrient deposit in a target hex, then moving the deposit to the next hex, the system steers growth along a desired path without direct mechanical manipulation. Nature finds the optimal path between crumbs.

Like a mouse following cheese crumbs.

In needle mode, the AI follows the last growth event, predicts the next hex on the string line, and pre-loads it.
In saturation mode, the AI activates the target hex plus its 6 neighbours to expand a growth front.

## Growth Sequence

1. **Maintenance:** Prevent scab, maintain moist clot, maintain ionic continuity.
2. **Pattern Initiation:** DNA-driven patterning resumes under bioelectric and chemical signalling.
3. **Needle Growth:** Vascular, neural, and skeletal lines are laid down first via crumb-guided chemotaxis.
4. **Saturation Growth:** Bulk tissue fills around lines via saturation delivery.
5. **Termination:** Growth stops when DNA pattern completion is signalled and nutrient demand falls. Cap removal exposes new epidermis to air, triggering keratinisation and natural sealing.

## Testable Predictions

1. A moist, ionically conductive, nutrient-circulating cap will show reduced scarring and prolonged blastema-like activity compared to dry dressing in a mammalian model.
2. Localised nutrient gradients delivered via a hexagonal array will steer neurite and vascular outgrowth in vitro in a predictable, crumb-following manner.
3. Needle-mode carbon scaffold deposition will increase organised bone formation compared to uniform calcium delivery alone.
4. External nutrient supply will reduce systemic calcium and protein loss during prolonged regeneration compared to unfed controls.

## Proposed Testing Pathway

**Phase 1, In Vitro:** Test chemotaxis and hex-guided growth on neuronal and endothelial cell cultures using a hexagonal microfluidic array.
**Phase 2, Ex Vivo:** Test cap maintenance of wound moisture, bioelectric continuity, and sterility on tissue models.
**Phase 3, Animal Model:** Test safety, infection control, nutrient balance, and growth patterns in an established amputation model under ethical oversight.
